Reviewer Comments:
Review-1:

Review-2: The paper can be accepted as an exploratory methodological study if its conclusions remain carefully bounded.\n 1. Explain the deterministic LLM-derived category mapping and provide manual-validation statistics.\n 2. Compare imputed marginal and joint distributions with observed-data distributions to detect implausible reconstruction.\n 3. Add cluster-profile uncertainty and avoid interpreting imputed variables as observed facts.\n 4. Analyze newspaper-source, year, and collection-method effects explicitly.\n 5. The absence of a population denominator means the dataset cannot estimate suicide incidence or prevalence; state this prominently.\n 6. Preserve responsible-reporting safeguards and avoid publishing details that could identify individuals or sensationalize methods of suicide.

Review-3: The paper is methodologically thoughtful in testing whether imputation improves downstream clustering and in evaluating robustness across 100,000 metric-weight combinations.\n 1. Because missingness is likely not at random, discuss how each imputation method may distort sensitive variables such as occupation, location, and circumstances.\n 2. Validate imputation quality through artificial masking experiments on fields with sufficient observed values.\n 3. The discovered two-cluster structure appears aligned with source cohorts/time periods; examine whether it reflects collection-process changes rather than meaningful epidemiological structure.\n 4. Report stability under bootstrap resampling and alternative numbers of clusters.\n 5. Keep internal clustering indices separate from evidence of substantive public-health validity.\n 6. Provide sensitivity analysis to the category-consolidation mapping from 918 raw values to 156 canonical categories.

Review-4: The manuscript addresses an important public-health data challenge and presents an interesting robustness analysis. However, serious concerns regarding data validity, missing-data assumptions, evaluation methodology, interpretability, and ethical safeguards prevent acceptance in its current form.

Newspaper-reported suicide cases are not representative of all suicide deaths. Reporting depends on geography, gender, age, social status, method, sensationalism, and access to media. Imputation cannot correct this fundamental selection bias.
The manuscript must explain how the 1,617 cases were identified, screened, deduplicated, and verified. Search terms, newspaper sources, inclusion criteria, inter-rater agreement, and procedures for resolving conflicting reports are required.
The newly collected data should be clearly distinguished from the existing public collection. The authors should report the number of cases contributed by each source and year and assess possible distributional differences between the original and extended datasets.
Missingness reaching 90% raises serious questions about recoverability. At this level, imputed values may reflect algorithmic assumptions more than observed evidence. Field-specific missingness rates and the proportion of cases affected should be reported.
The missing-data mechanism is not examined. MissForest, MICE, and KNN do not automatically provide valid estimates under Missing Not at Random conditions, which are highly plausible in newspaper reporting.
